# Market memo - the AI capex cycle and NVIDIA expectations

**Research cut-off:** 6 October 2026  
**Market-data date:** 6 October 2026  
**Status:** Educational research only. Not investment advice or a recommendation.

## Executive view

Reported evidence supports a powerful AI-infrastructure investment cycle:
Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon and Meta increased combined raw purchases of
property and equipment by **64.5%** across their latest reported fiscal
periods, while NVIDIA's FY2026 Data Center revenue grew **68.2%**. The more
interesting Markets conclusion is that execution alone does not determine the
share-price response. NVIDIA exceeded the high end of its own revenue range in
each of the three reported quarters in the guidance bridge, but two of the
three next-day reactions underperformed QQQ. The next release should therefore
be assessed through the *change in forward expectations*: revenue guide,
China assumption, gross-margin outlook and evidence that hyperscaler spending
can remain funded.

The observation is not that strong results are negative for the stock. Rather,
the variation in relative reactions is consistent with the market comparing
reported execution and forward commentary with a much higher, changing
expectations bar.

## Catalysts to monitor

1. **Forward revenue guide:** whether the next midpoint and range extend,
   maintain or reset the current trajectory matters more than a backward-looking
   growth headline.
2. **Data Center demand breadth:** the capex thesis becomes more resilient if
   demand is evidenced across cloud, sovereign, enterprise and other customers,
   rather than relying on a narrow part of the ecosystem.
3. **Hyperscaler capacity to fund investment:** revenue demand is more durable
   when higher infrastructure investment remains supported by operating cash
   flow; this is why the project retains the cash-flow and P&E-purchase screen.
4. **China assumption:** management's FY2027 Q1, Q2 and Q3 outlooks assumed no
   Data Center compute revenue from China. Any change to that assumption is a
   material item for the addressable market and forward numbers.

## Risks to monitor

1. **Expectation risk:** an own-guidance beat can still disappoint if investors
   had expected a larger beat or a stronger next-quarter guide.
2. **Product-transition and margin risk:** platform ramps can alter mix and
   supply economics. Revenue growth should be read alongside the gross-margin
   outlook, not in isolation.
3. **Investment-cycle risk:** hyperscaler P&E growth does not prove that every
   dollar is AI-specific, nor that spending remains at the same rate.
4. **Policy and geographic risk:** the explicit exclusion of China Data Center
   compute revenue from recent guidance makes regulatory and market-access
   developments an observable risk factor rather than a peripheral detail.

## Diligence conclusion

The current evidence is consistent with a strong AI-infrastructure cycle and
with NVIDIA delivering above its own near-term revenue ranges. It does not,
however, establish valuation attractiveness or predict the next share-price
move. The highest-value next question is: **does NVIDIA's next guidance change
the market's implied revenue, margin or China assumptions more than the market
already expects?**
